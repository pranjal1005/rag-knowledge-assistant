Vector Embeddings: Turning Text into Searchable Numbers
A vector embedding is a fixed-length list of numbers that represents the meaning of a piece of text, produced by a neural network trained so that texts with similar meaning end up close together in that numeric space, and texts with different meaning end up far apart. "The cat sat on the mat" and "a feline rested on the rug" would have embeddings close to each other despite sharing almost no words, because the model has learned meaning, not just vocabulary overlap -- which is exactly what keyword search (like SQL LIKE or a basic inverted index) cannot do.

Distance between two embeddings is usually measured with cosine similarity, which looks at the angle between the two vectors rather than their raw magnitude, so it's insensitive to text length in a way that raw Euclidean distance is not. A cosine similarity near 1 means the texts are semantically very close; near 0 means unrelated; negative values (with models that produce them) mean semantically opposed.

Embedding models range from small, fast, locally-runnable models (like the MiniLM family, roughly 80-400MB, running comfortably on CPU) to large, hosted models accessed via API (OpenAI's text-embedding-3, Voyage AI's models) that tend to capture more nuance, especially on technical or domain-specific text, at the cost of a per-call price and network latency. The choice is a real engineering tradeoff, not just a quality question: local models mean zero marginal cost and no external dependency for the highest-volume part of a RAG pipeline (embedding every chunk of every document), while hosted models mean better retrieval quality at the cost of money and a network round-trip for every single chunk during indexing. A common pattern is to start with a local model to validate the pipeline cheaply, then swap to a hosted model if retrieval quality turns out to be the bottleneck once the system is under real use.
